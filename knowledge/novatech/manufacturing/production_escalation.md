# Production Escalation Procedure

Department: Manufacturing Operations
Category: procedure
Classification: internal
Version: 2026.2
Effective date: 2026-03-01
Owner: Manufacturing Operations Head

## 1. When to escalate

Escalate when a production failure threatens safety, a customer commitment, or output for the current shift, or when the response target in the Machine Failure SOP is missed.

## 2. Escalation ladder

| Level | Trigger | Contact | Response time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Machine stopped, operator cannot resolve | Shift supervisor | Immediate |
| L2 | Not restored in 30 minutes, or high severity | Maintenance lead + production planner | 15 minutes |
| L3 | Not restored in 2 hours, or line stopped | Manufacturing Operations head + Engineering Manager | 30 minutes |
| L4 | Not restored in 4 hours, customer delivery at risk | Plant Director + Quality Manager + Finance for expedited spend | 1 hour |
| L5 | Safety event, environmental release or multi-day stoppage | Managing Director, crisis team convened | Immediate |

## 3. Communication

Each escalation level posts an update in the production channel with: machine ID, severity, impact on orders, actions taken, expected restoration time and owner. Updates repeat at least every 30 minutes until closure.

## 4. Decision authority during escalation

- Stopping a line for safety: any employee, no approval needed.
- Restarting after a safety stop: Safety officer plus shift supervisor.
- Overtime to recover output: department head.
- Expedited freight or emergency spares: per the Approval Matrix.

## 5. Closure

The escalation closes when production is restored and quality is confirmed. A written summary is circulated within 24 hours for L3 and above, and root-cause actions are tracked to completion.
